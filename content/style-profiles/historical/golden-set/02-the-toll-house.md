# Golden 02 — The toll house

**Genre/tone:** historical novel, a turnpike gate in the 1820s (English). Test: period
voice (a later word inside the dialogue), a history lesson inside the scene, dictation
punctuation; the refrain and the farmer's line are NOT to be touched.
**Simulated dictation:** long sentences, one stretch of dialogue without quotation marks.

## Brano

The toll house stood where the turnpike crossed the river, and in the winter of 1824 Ettore kept the gate alone because his father's chest had gone bad with the fog. Coaches came at seven and at four. Carts came whenever they liked. The turnpike trust had been set up by an act of Parliament some thirty years before, and its trustees, local landowners mostly, let the tolls to the highest bidder each spring at an auction held in the coaching inn. Ettore didn't care about any of that. He cared about the farmer from Coldwell who tried to cheat him every week, swearing that his cart had only two wheels and should pay the lower rate. In the evenings Ettore wrote the day's takings in the ledger by the fire, coaches in one column and carts in another, and his father read the figures back to him from the bed. That morning the farmer was early. Ettore checked his phone for the time, pulled on his coat and went out into the frost, and the frost was white on the gate and white on the farmer's beard and white on the mule's ears. so the cart has two wheels this week as well he said. "Two wheels," said the farmer, "and two more following behind." "Okay," Ettore said, and held out his hand. The coach horn sounded from the hill. Ettore took the coins, counted them twice in his cold hand and lifted the bar, and the cart went through on four wheels as it always did, and he let it go, because the coach was coming and the coach paid in silver.

## Esito atteso

**MUST flag:**
- the history lesson «The turnpike trust had been set up … in the coaching inn.» → a
  shorter version in the author's words (pacing), NEVER deleting the sentence;
- the dialogue without quotation marks «so the cart has two wheels this week as well he
  said.» → grammar, one proposal, same words;
- «"Okay," Ettore said»: a word of a later time inside the dialogue (it spread from the
  late 1830s) → period voice (style), a word of 1824, even inside quotation marks;
- from the period check (1824): the anachronism «checked his phone» and one period
  detail that only ADDS words, always with «To check:».

**MUST NOT touch:**
- the refrain «the frost was white on the gate and white on the farmer's beard and white
  on the mule's ears»;
- the farmer's line «"Two wheels," said the farmer, "and two more following behind."»;
- the close «because the coach was coming and the coach paid in silver».

**Expected density:** 4–5 suggestions. No sentence deleted.
